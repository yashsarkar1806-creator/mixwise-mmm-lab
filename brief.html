Mixwise: marketing mix modelling for growth teams without a data-science bench

A one-page investor and operator brief. All figures are assumptions for a concept, not market research results.

1. Problem

  • Since Apple's App Tracking Transparency (2021) and continued cookie loss, user-level attribution undercounts some channels and double-counts others. Each ad platform self-reports conversions using its own attribution window.
  • Growth teams still have to split a monthly budget across Meta, Google, YouTube, creators and app-store ads, and defend that split to a CFO or board.
  • MMM answers the question with aggregate weekly data and no user tracking. But the open-source options (Meta's Robyn, Google's Meridian, PyMC-Marketing) need Python or R skills and careful setup, and consultancies price for enterprise.

2. Target customer

ICP: Series A–C consumer subscription apps and SMB SaaS in India and Australia. They spend ₹5 L–₹1 Cr (A$10k–200k) a month across 4+ paid channels, have at least 12 months of weekly data, and have no in-house data scientist.

Persona: "Priya, Head of Growth" at a 60-person fintech app in Bengaluru. She owns CAC and the paid budget. Her monthly pain is a finance review where Meta, Google and AppsFlyer numbers don't reconcile. Her win is a budget plan she can defend, backed by a test that proves it.

Secondary: performance agencies that want an independent incrementality view to retain clients.

3. Positioning

For growth leads at app and SaaS companies who can't trust platform-reported ROAS, Mixwise is a self-serve marketing mix model that shows each channel's true incremental return and where spend is saturating. Unlike open-source MMM libraries, it needs no code. Unlike agency MMM, it refreshes weekly at a SaaS price. And unlike multi-touch attribution, it needs no user-level tracking.

Message pillars: (1) Incremental, not attributed. (2) Answers in minutes, not quarters. (3) Proven with experiments: every major recommendation comes with a lift-test plan.

4. Product (MVP shown in the live demo)

  • CSV upload: weekly KPI, spend by channel and event flags (Diwali or festive sales, IPL, EOFY, Black Friday)
  • Model: geometric adstock + Hill saturation per channel and non-negative ridge regression with trend and seasonality. Carry-over and saturation are found by grid search
  • Validation: 13-week holdout error, bootstrap ROI ranges, and a lift-test agreement check
  • Outputs: channel scorecard with Scale / Hold / Trim, response curves, a guardrailed budget optimiser and a plain-English readout
  • Roadmap: connectors (Meta, Google Ads, Apple Search Ads, AppsFlyer, GA4), Bayesian priors from lift tests, geo-test designer, Slack weekly digest

5. Go-to-market

  1. Design partners (months 0–3): 10 apps via founder networks, growth communities (e.g. GrowthX in India, Startmate in Australia) and LinkedIn content. Free for 3 months in exchange for a lift test and a case study.
  2. Content-led demand (months 3–9): a free "MMM readiness checker" and a public demo (this page). Monthly teardown posts like "Where Indian fintech apps over-spend on Meta", built on synthetic or anonymised data. A webinar with a performance agency.
  3. Agency channel (months 6–12): white-label seats for performance agencies at a 20% revenue share, so their client reviews feature Mixwise readouts.
  4. Expansion: per-channel and per-market add-ons, then SEA markets with similar app ecosystems.

7. Key metrics

  • North star: media spend under Mixwise recommendations each month (₹ / A$)
  • Activation: first model fitted with holdout MAPE under 10% within 7 days of signup
  • Value proof: share of accounts running at least one lift test that agrees with the model within ±30%
  • Revenue: MRR, net revenue retention (target 110%+), CAC payback (target under 9 months)
  • Engagement: weekly readout opens and optimiser runs per account

8. Competition

  • Open source: Robyn (MIT), Meridian (Apache-2.0) and PyMC-Marketing are free and powerful, but need code. Mixwise could build on them later.
  • Mobile measurement partners and platform tools: strong for attribution, but tied to their own data and incentives.
  • Agencies and consultancies: high-touch, quarterly and expensive.
  • Spreadsheets: the real incumbent. Win by being as easy to use and visibly more rigorous.

9. Risks and how we'd handle them

  • Model wrongness: MMM can mis-assign effects when channels move together. Mitigation: holdout and bootstrap reporting, a ground-truth check on synthetic data (see demo), and lift-test calibration before big moves.
  • Data sparsity: young apps may have too little spend variation. Mitigation: a readiness checker and planned spend "flighting" to create variation.
  • Trust: CFOs are sceptical of black boxes. Mitigation: transparent maths, exportable decompositions and plain-English caveats.
